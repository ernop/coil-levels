A guide to board characterization

A board is a vector of measurements and distributions. No aggregate “style score” or difficulty score is assigned here. These descriptors use only walls and open cells; the saved solution is used for validity checks.

1 · Occupancy

Open cells ÷ total cells. High occupancy means little wall area, but two boards can have equal occupancy and different arrangements. The pair below has the same 50% occupancy.

Metric illustrations only; these are not claimed to be valid Coil puzzles.

2 · Texture and density variation

Variance of open fractions in nonoverlapping 32×32 tiles. Low variance means local occupancy is fairly uniform. High variance means denser and emptier areas coexist. Partial edge tiles count equally with full tiles.

This differs from the overlapping 3×3/5×5 window statistic in the Python prototype. Scale matters: a 32-cell window measures the same cell scale on every board, but a different fraction of its total area.

3 · Open runs and orientation

A run is a maximal uninterrupted horizontal or vertical stretch of open cells. The histogram counts runs, not cells. The comparison’s mean-run value averages the horizontal and vertical means. A run is not necessarily a legal solution segment.

Axis bias = (horizontal open adjacencies − vertical open adjacencies) ÷ all open adjacencies. +1 means entirely horizontal; −1 entirely vertical. A quarter-turn swaps run distributions and reverses the sign.

4 · Wall shapes and open squares

Largest all-wall/all-open square is reported as its side length in cells, with an exact top-left coordinate witness in the JSON. The explorer also provides its side as a percentage of the board’s side for comparisons across sizes. Its spectrum counts every possible placement at each side length; placements overlap.

A large all-open square is a geometric observation, not a room decomposition. A long thin wall can have a large area and a largest square of side one.

5 · Pillars, degrees, and interfaces

Isolated-wall fraction: wall cells with no orthogonally adjacent wall, divided by all wall cells. Degree: number of orthogonal open neighbours; degree-two fraction divides by open cells.

Interface per open cell = (4N − 2E) ÷ N. Exterior boundaries count as wall-facing sides. Degree two does not by itself force both edges into a solution: a cell may be an endpoint.

6 · Boundary and symmetry

The edge profile reports occupancy at each exact distance from the nearest border. Edge contrast subtracts overall occupancy from the outermost layer’s occupancy.

Adjusted symmetry = (observed agreement − chance) ÷ (1 − chance), where chance = p²+(1−p)². Zero is the independent-density baseline, not “no structure”; one is exact invariance. Uniform boards have undefined adjusted agreement. Reflections and half-turns are reported separately.

Geometry is not a solving proof. The compact C# measurements scale to 100 million cells. The Python structural-analysis prototype additionally studies cuts, rooms, chamber cores, deductions, and unresolved constraints, with a default 100,000-cell bound. Those proof metrics have not been computed for this large-board collection. Motif entropy, multiscale chambers, and room signatures are further exploration opportunities; they are not silently approximated here.

What the knobs actually do

Tweak geometry

Len1 is the offset into the existing segment where a tweak starts. Len2 is the perpendicular excursion. Len3 is the parallel part. Names that combine these usually maximize an expression over candidates.

len1 favors later pivots. len23 favors excursion + parallel length. sz2-3 favors perpendicular over parallel length. They are preferences, not guaranteed final board traits.

Random, limited, and mixed choices

rndN scores each candidate with a random integer; ties retain the first maximum, so it is not uniform sampling of candidates. 1stlast2 chooses between extremes of length ordering. equal23 favors equal excursion and parallel lengths.

2lim10 gives a score bonus below a length threshold; that is distinct from an enforced geometric cap or --lim, which limits candidate enumeration.

Segment ordering

First and Last choose earlier/later segments in the evolving known path. Longest prioritizes segment length. Weighted variants combine path index and length; successive roots reduce index influence.

Changing the order changes which future tweaks become available. It can interact strongly with the tweak picker.

Initial walk and stopping

The generator starts with a random path, then tweaks it. --wander-full makes initial moves use the full available length. --wander-max limits initial move length; --wander-steps limits its segment count.

--loops 1 limits segment-picker passes. It does not mean one tweak. --keep-deadends keeps the generated endpoints instead of trimming their one-exit pockets.

Compatibility quirks preserved and exposed: 2lim25 appears twice with identical definitions. len23-10th appears twice with different probabilities; the CLI selects the first definition, which uses a 1-in-5 bonus. The second definition is unreachable by that name. The study covers 53 distinct selectable names, not every historical name in the older web dropdown.

How the large representatives were chosen

The baseline rnd99 and full initial-slide variant anchor the selection. Six further configurations maximize distance from already chosen configuration means across ten range-normalized geometry features (maximum run length is log-transformed first). Each contributes its most separated observed seed pair through 2,000 square; 5,000 uses one seed per selected configuration; 10,000 includes four boards across three configurations, including a same-method seed pair.

The unlimited-candidate last-picker recipe became costly at 500 square (52–122 seconds per export). From 1,000 upward it is replaced by the measured finite-candidate seg-2lim10-Last alternative. Its interrupted 1,000-square run produced no claimed specimen. The short-tweak equal23short/First export then took 242 seconds at 2,000 square; the separately surveyed equal23short/Longest was tried at 5,000. Four ordering-heavy 5,000-square experiments reached their recorded 10- or 20-minute budgets and have no claimed specimen. Additional Weighted4/lim20 recipes replace them in the saved collection; the outcomes remain in the selection record. The extra len23/Weighted4 probe completed in 104 seconds at 5,000, so it supplies the dense 10,000-square specimen. These measured scaling decisions are saved in the selection record. This is a reproducible coverage heuristic. Feature scaling affects selection, and three seeds do not establish a recipe’s full range.
